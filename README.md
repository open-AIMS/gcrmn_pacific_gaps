# gcrmn_pacific_gaps
Code repository for the analysis of monitoring metadata, compiled by the GCRMN, to identify priority knowledge gaps.

## Purpose


## Background
The Pacific region hosts 65,350 km2 of coral reefs, representing the second-largest region in the world, with 26.2% of the world’s coral reef extent. Spanning over 23 countries and territories, these ecosystems are deeply interwoven with the cultural identity, livelihoods and food security of a large proportion of the population. However, coral reefs are on the frontline of climate change, and future projections suggest these ecosystems may be severely compromised in the absence of significant curbs on greenhouse gas emissions and effective management of local pressures (e.g., water pollution, unsustainable fishing). Under this scenario, coral reef monitoring is a cornerstone for informing adaptive management practices, as it is impossible to manage what we don’t measure.
Recent monitoring reports indicate that coral reefs in the region have maintained high levels of hard coral cover over the past four decades, a key indicator of their condition. However, since 2010, increasing global pressures from marine heatwaves and localised stressors (e.g., Crown-of-Thorn Starfish outbreaks, large cyclones) have led to an 18.3% decline in coral cover across the region, relative to a historical reference period (1980-2009). While detectable declines across a vast region such as the Pacific are of significant concern, it is important to recognise that strong variability has also been documented in the magnitude and ecological responses to these pressures across the Pacific. Hard coral cover has decreased in the three northern Pacific subregions but remained stable or increased in the south. This reflects the region's vast size, diverse benthic communities, and varied disturbance regimes. Differences in stressor timing, intensity, and frequency—such as thermal stress, cyclones, or Crown-of-Thorn Starfish outbreaks—lead to asynchronous benthic responses across subregions.
In addition to the heterogeneity of pressures and ecological responses across a vast range of coral reef habitats in the Pacific, coral reef monitoring efforts remain highly variable in space and time and are not necessarily aligned with the extent of coral reefs across countries. Such discrepancies in data availability introduce uncertainty into the assessment of the temporal and spatial trajectories of coral reefs, curtailing the effectiveness of monitoring efforts to inform policy and management priorities across the Pacific. This study builds on efforts from the GCRMN in the compilation and analysis of monitoring data across the world to expand on the analysis of monitoring efforts in the Pacific and provide recommendations to enhance the spatial and temporal representativeness of monitoring in the region and thereby strengthen the value of monitoring to inform regional and national policies and management practices for coral reef conservation.


## Approach

Building on the upcoming GCRMN report "Status of Coral Reefs of the world 2025" (Gonzalez-Rivero et al 2026), this study used of monitoring metadata (i.e., location and temporal coverage) to assess the representativeness of monitoring across spatial domains across the Pacific (Fig. 1). Coral reef extent from the WCMC dataset was used to assess the spatial representativeness of monitoring, and the length of the temporal series was used to evaluate the temporal coverage of monitoring across spatial representative units (e.g., GCRMN Subregions and Marine Ecoregions of the World).
